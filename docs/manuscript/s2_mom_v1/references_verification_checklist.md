## C. Jain (2026) and the positioning statement

**What was inspected.**
- SSRN abstract page and file link: HTTP 403 on 2026-10-07 (also 403 when the protocol was written).
- The abstract as indexed by web search: point-in-time top-500 Indian universes with dead names retained; paired honest-versus-survivor backtests; 2 × 2 design (vintages 2013 and 2015; two survivor definitions); bias of +0.8 to +3.3 percentage points per year; terminal-wealth inflation of +7% to +38%; exits are not uniformly losers. It does not mention momentum, a return-sorted strategy, transaction costs, corporate actions, a correction ladder or a held-back period.
- The author's public repository (`github.com/Finance-broski/backtest-bias`, which names Ayan Jain as its author and links SSRN 7099378): it describes the result as "Survivor-only universes inflated equal-weight returns by +0.8 to +3.2 pp/yr". The word "momentum" does not appear on that page. (The repository says 3.2 where the abstract says 3.3; the manuscript uses the abstract's figure.)
- A second paper by the same author, SSRN 7423179 ("Selection Look-Ahead Is Not a Data Problem", September 2026), was found during the search. Its abstract concerns a pairs-trading screen on US stocks and ETFs. It is not about Indian momentum, was not part of the literature assembled for this project, and is not cited.

**Conclusion.** The available evidence indicates that Jain (2026) measures survivorship bias in universe-level (equal-weight) returns, which is not a material overlap with the A–D ladder on a momentum design. This is an indication from the abstract and the author's own description, **not a verification of the full text. The citation stays UNRESOLVED.**

**Effect on the manuscript.**
- The bounded statement is kept in its approved form: "Within the literature reviewed for this study, we did not identify a directly comparable design combining the specific A–D correction ladder, the separate transaction-cost layer, and an untouched chronological confirmation sample."
- The text states that the statement rests on full texts for two of the nine Indian papers and abstracts for seven, that no exclusive claim is made against Jain (2026), and that the question remains unresolved.
- No sentence in the manuscript says that no earlier study exists.
